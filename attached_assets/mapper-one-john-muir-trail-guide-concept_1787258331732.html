The trail in context

A Sierra trail with a much bigger story.

The John Muir Trail begins in Yosemite and is one of the best-known long-distance routes in the Sierra Nevada. This mapper.one page, however, is intentionally narrower: it represents a 24-mile route geometry within Yosemite National Park supplied with the trail record used by mapper.one.

For planning context, the National Park Service describes the John Muir Trail from Happy Isles to Tuolumne Meadows as a 22-mile one-way hike with 5,900 feet of elevation gain. NPS says that route passes Vernal and Nevada Falls, runs below the south face of Half Dome, crosses Sunrise Meadow, passes Cathedral Peak, and descends to Tuolumne Meadows.

Why the numbers do not perfectly match mapper.one’s supplied route record is 24 miles. The NPS Happy Isles–Tuolumne route description is 22 miles. Because the supplied mapper.one record does not identify its exact endpoints in the public page data, this guide does not assume the two geometries are identical or attach NPS’s 5,900-foot elevation gain to the 24-mile record.
Official route context

What hikers encounter on the Yosemite JMT.

NPS identifies Happy Isles as the beginning of the John Muir Trail. The Yosemite Valley section climbs toward some of the park’s most recognizable scenery, including the Vernal and Nevada Fall corridor, before continuing into the high country.

Permits & access

Overnight trips require planning.

Wilderness permits are required for overnight wilderness travel.

For hikers continuing over Donohue Pass on the full JMT, Yosemite currently limits eligible exits and specifies which trailheads can be used. Permit rules, quotas, roads, shuttle access and seasonal conditions can change, so the current Yosemite National Park guidance should be checked before every trip.

NPS currently identifies Happy Isles to Past LYV (Donohue Pass eligible) and Lyell Canyon (Donohue Pass eligible) as the two trailheads that provide access to the full JMT beyond Yosemite. NPS also states that the Donohue Pass exit quota is 45 people per day.

Trailhead note

Getting to Happy Isles.

NPS recommends reaching the Mist and John Muir Trailhead using the Valleywide Shuttle or East Valley Shuttle. There is no parking directly at the trailhead; the closest parking is at Curry Village, about one mile away.

Seasonal reality matters. Spring runoff can create heavy spray and slippery footing around the waterfall corridor. High-country snow, wildfire, road access and weather can all materially change a Yosemite itinerary. Use this guide as orientation, then verify current conditions with NPS.
FAQ

John Muir Trail questions.

Is this the entire John Muir Trail?

No. This page represents a 24-mile mapped trail record within Yosemite National Park. The complete JMT continues well beyond Yosemite.

Where does the John Muir Trail begin?

The National Park Service identifies Happy Isles in Yosemite Valley as the beginning of the John Muir Trail.

How far is the JMT from Happy Isles to Tuolumne Meadows?

NPS lists the Happy Isles-to-Tuolumne Meadows JMT route as 22 miles one way with 5,900 feet of elevation gain.

Do I need a permit?

A wilderness permit is required for overnight wilderness travel. JMT thru-hikers continuing over Donohue Pass must follow Yosemite’s specific trailhead eligibility and quota rules.
